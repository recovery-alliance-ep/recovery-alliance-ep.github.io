Be prepared

Know the signs of an opioid overdose

  • Won't wake up or respond to their name or a firm rub on the chest
  • Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing
  • Gurgling or snoring sounds
  • Blue or gray lips and fingertips
  • Tiny, pinpoint pupils
  • Cold, clammy skin

Not sure? Give naloxone.

It won't hurt someone who isn't having an opioid overdose.

Respond with confidence

What to do

  1. Call 911.
  2. Give naloxone — follow the directions on the package (one spray in one nostril for nasal spray).
  3. Support breathing if you're trained, or turn them on their side (recovery position).
  4. Stay with them. If there's no response after 2–3 minutes, give another dose if you have one.
  5. Naloxone wears off — they can stop breathing again. Stay until help arrives.
